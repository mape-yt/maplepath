Community timelines · Method 5

Understand the numbers.

Historical context from other contributors, with visible limits.

Test data stays separate

During testing, MaplePath shows no community averages or estimates. Test records and older records without a source label are excluded. Editing dates does not change that label. When real community collection begins, only new eligible submissions will contribute; switching it on will not turn test history into real evidence.

What counts

Each saved application has its own timeline. Starting a separate application keeps earlier attempts available; editing details or dates updates the existing attempt. Matching waiting times from different applications still count separately. Existing timelines remain available without rewriting their history.

Each comparison uses the same roadmap and step, with completions from the last 730 days. Matching dates or durations count separately: common waiting times should appear as often as people report them. Separate saved timelines from the same account also count. Your own records are excluded from your comparison.

We show both the number of timelines and the number of distinct contributors. Completed timelines from at least five other contributors are needed to display durations. Editing a saved timeline updates that observation; it does not create another contribution. If the same saved record is read twice, we count it once. We do not merge separate records because their contents match.

You can save progress without remembering the dates and add them later. Missing dates stay blank; completed steps need both dates before they contribute a duration. We never substitute today or a zero-day wait for an unknown date. An unfinished step with an unknown start still counts as unfinished when checking whether an estimate is appropriate.

Invalid, reversed or future dates are excluded, allowing up to 24 hours for the date editor’s local-noon convention. Durations are recalculated from the dates, rounded to whole days with a one-day minimum. Editing a date changes the statistics the next time they are loaded. Your saved records are never deleted by this process.

Typical duration and unusual values

The main number is the median: the middle duration. The range covers the middle 50% of valid completed durations. Both retain unusually long and short cases.

The average includes every eligible completed timeline, including unusually short or long durations. With at least ten distinct contributors, values below the lower quartile minus three interquartile ranges, or above the upper quartile plus three interquartile ranges, are flagged for context only. The check uses a one-day minimum spread when many dates are identical. Unusual does not mean false: a flag never removes a duration from the average, median or historical range.

Location groups

Permit comparisons can separate new permits from extensions and use your location when you submitted, entered in Application details. Missing details appear only in All types or All locations; we never substitute your current location. Other pathways still use location when a step was first recorded, which is not necessarily the application submission location. PNP streams already have separate province-specific roadmaps.

Only useful application details

Your selected roadmap supplies the program and stream. Optional details record the submission date, result and decision or withdrawal date. Permits also have a new-permit/extension choice and location at submission. For PNP, the application dates and result refer to the provincial application; the roadmap continues through the federal stages. Provincial approval is not permanent residence. Details are stored once per application, not copied onto every step. No government application numbers or document uploads are requested.

Remaining-time estimates

For an active waiting or IRCC step, we look at other cases completed in the last 180 days that took longer than your wait so far. Those cases must come from at least 20 distinct other contributors. Their middle 50% of remaining durations forms the displayed range. Matching durations and valid unusually long cases are kept in this comparison.

We withhold estimates for preparation steps, invitation or selection rounds, work-authorization checklists, general open-work-permit and LMIA-exempt guides that mix categories, unknown or unmatched permit submission locations or application types, and samples with at least as many contributors with ongoing timelines as contributors with recent completions. Estimates stop when a result is recorded, except that provincial approval can still be followed by federal steps. We also withhold them when too few comparable contributors remain. Sample thresholds are MaplePath safeguards, not proof of statistical accuracy.

This is a historical comparison, not a trained ML model or a calibrated probability of your decision date. Completed cases can underrepresent long waits, and unfinished cases are not modelled. Users may record steps differently, and location alone does not capture every factor. A large sample is not automatically a reliable one.

Community reports and official sources

Community dates are self-reported. We check consistency, but do not independently verify applications or identify every fabricated report. Every eligible timeline has equal weight, so accounts with more timelines can influence the result more. Minimum sample sizes count distinct accounts, but that does not prevent one person from making multiple accounts. Record counts are not verified counts of unique immigration applications.

IRCC’s estimates cover whole application processes and can depend on application type and location. They are shown as a separate official reference, not mixed into an average of individual community steps. No online forum posts or unverified third-party numbers are added to your community sample.
